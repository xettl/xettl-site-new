The Xettl Newsletter · Edition 02

The bank account question, answered

Which providers let you open a U.S. account without a Social Security number? CFPB guidance explains that an SSN is not the only identification number a financial institution may accept. The harder question is which provider can verify you before you land.

For someone who is not a U.S. person, customer-identification rules allow other identifying information, such as a passport number and country of issuance. Institutions can still decide which documents, applicants, and account-opening methods they accept.

That is why one branch may accept a passport and ITIN while one online flow cannot verify a recent U.S. address. “No SSN required” is not the same as “guaranteed online approval before arrival.”

If the online application says no

One common obstacle is that an automated system cannot match a recent U.S. address to the applicant's identity. That does not necessarily mean you are ineligible.

  1. Ask whether the issue involves identity, address verification, documentation, eligibility, or something else. The provider may not always disclose the exact reason.
  2. Ask whether a branch visit changes the outcome. In-person verification can succeed where an automated address check fails.
  3. If an ITIN application is in progress, ask whether the provider can open the account once it is issued rather than starting the whole process over.
  4. Try a second provider before assuming the problem is you. Document requirements genuinely differ by institution, not just by policy on paper.
This week's action: Make a document folder with your passport, status documents, move evidence, and address evidence. Share it only through an official provider channel.
