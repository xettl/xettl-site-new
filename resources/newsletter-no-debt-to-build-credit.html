You don't need debt to build credit

If someone has told you to carry a balance so it "looks like you are using credit," know this: carrying an interest-bearing balance can cost you money without creating a special credit-building benefit. Paying on time matters; paying interest does not help by itself.

A card issuer may report your account, limit, balance, and payment status to credit bureaus. Paying on time helps build a positive record. Carrying a balance from one month to the next usually adds interest; it does not create a special credit-building advantage.

A debit card is different. It spends money from your deposit account and usually does not report borrowing activity. That makes debit useful for access and budgeting, but not normally for establishing a credit file.

What "low utilization" actually looks like

Utilization is the number that trips people up most, so here it is in concrete terms: a secured card with a $500 limit and a $45 reported balance is 9% utilization. That is below the 30% ceiling many experts discuss and within the lower range some experts prefer. No single percentage guarantees a score. The balance reported is often your statement balance, but timing varies by issuer.

Paying down a balance before it is reported can help keep utilization low. You can also ask whether you may qualify for a higher limit—and whether the request involves a hard credit inquiry. The issuer may decline the request.

This week's action: If you are considering a secured card, compare the fee, required deposit, bureau reporting, upgrade path, and deposit-refund rules before applying.
