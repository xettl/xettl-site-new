Account access · Practical guide

Can you open a U.S. bank account without an SSN?

An SSN is not the only identification path federal rules allow—but each institution decides which documents, applicants, and account-opening methods it accepts.

The short answer: The CFPB says you do not need a Social Security number to get a bank or credit-union account. A provider may use an ITIN or, for a non-U.S. person, another government-issued identification number. Each institution still sets a risk-based verification process and product requirements.

Why answers differ

Financial institutions must form a reasonable belief that they know a customer's identity. That does not create one universal document list. A bank may accept a foreign passport, consular identification, an ITIN, or another government-issued document, while another may require a different combination or restrict online opening.

Remote systems often rely on U.S. address and public-record checks. Someone who recently moved may be unable to pass those checks even when a branch can verify the same person with physical documents.

Documents worth preparing

  • Unexpired passport and applicable visa or status documents.
  • ITIN, if you have one and the provider accepts it.
  • Proof of your current U.S. address, such as a lease or recent bill, if requested.
  • School enrollment, employment, or other move documentation when relevant to the provider's process.
  • Opening deposit and a payment method the institution accepts.

Ask these questions before applying

  1. Can a non-U.S. citizen open this exact account?
  2. Which identification numbers and documents are accepted without an SSN?
  3. Can the process begin online, or will I need to visit a branch?
  4. Is a U.S. residential address required at application time?
  5. What monthly fee, minimum balance, overdraft setting, and ATM costs apply?
  6. Is the institution federally insured, and how can I verify that status?

What “no SSN required” does not promise

It does not guarantee approval, pre-arrival opening, interest eligibility, a debit card on the same day, or access to every product. It also does not mean that an ITIN is available to everyone; the IRS issues ITINs for federal tax purposes to people who meet its rules.

Prepare, then verify. Bring more than one form of identification, use the provider's official contact information, and ask for the exact product's requirements before submitting personal documents.
